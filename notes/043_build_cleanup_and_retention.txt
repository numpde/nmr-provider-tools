Build cleanup and retention
===========================

Temporary build inputs must clean themselves up through their owning Make
workflow. Remove source captures, selection manifests, and disposable copies
after success, ordinary failure, or cancellation once their local readers and
mounted workers have stopped. Cleanup must preserve the original failure.

A streamed build passes bytes to Docker over stdin. After both producer and
client have finished, an uncertain daemon outcome does not require retaining
the source files: the daemon has no host mount through which to read them.
Do not confuse this with a container that still mounts the captured files.

Caller-selected release artifacts, configured deployment pins, and small image
ownership receipts have a separate lifetime. Retain what the corresponding
deployment or image-retirement owner actually uses, rather than keeping all
build scratch beside it. Temporary publication copies are disposable; their
reviewed preparation directory remains the artifact authority.

Deferred limits
---------------

An interrupted stream whose local producer has not been confirmed stopped
must retain its inputs. A still-needed network proxy or unresolved mounted
worker must also remain owned until reconciled. Keep these exceptions narrow
and report them explicitly; do not retain ordinary failed builds indefinitely.

SIGKILL and host failure can bypass EXIT cleanup. Recovery of abandoned work
needs its owner's evidence of stopped readers; this change does not introduce
a blanket age-based /tmp sweep. Engine build-cache GC and owned-image
retirement are separate from temporary-file cleanup.

Owned image retirement
----------------------

Each repository retires its own positively identified obsolete outputs. The
shared repository exposes make packages/images/retire with an explicit Docker
context and invokes the same owner after successful checker, delivery and
wheelhouse-tool image publication, once its build lock and inputs are released.
An already-busy image owner defers automatic cleanup without failing the build.

Preserve all container image references, current input/recipe selections,
explicit staged image IDs, and exact retained build IID receipts. Unknown
ownership, digest references, operation base tags and ambiguous multiple aliases
remain untouched. Legacy incomplete labels preserve that image rather than
blocking other positively owned candidates.

Creation time is not a retention signal. An obsolete positively owned image
is immediately eligible once its builder has retired and its current selection,
container, staged-ID, retained-receipt, and alias protections have been checked.
The current checker remains available for subsequent offline repository tests.

Removal uses exact immutable IDs without force or parent pruning, with fresh
borrower/alias checks under the maintained checkout lock. Uncoordinated manual
tag changes are outside that lock; this is not an adversarial Docker-client
sandbox. In particular, no generic engine-wide image or volume prune replaces
the repositories' deployment and image ownership knowledge.
